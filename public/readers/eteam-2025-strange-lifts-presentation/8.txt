Is dAp ever unbounded?





   We claim that there are infinitely many p that satisfy this property.
     In fact, all p : p ≥2, p ∈N satisfy this property.
   As proved in 4a), every natural number has a unique base p
    representation for p ∈N.
    So, consider the number
    pk −1 = (p −1)pk−1 + (p −1)pk−2 + · · · + (p −1).
   dA(N) = k(p −1) →∞as k →∞. So, dAp is unbounded as
     desired.

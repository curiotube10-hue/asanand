Generalised Bertrand’s Postulate                                                       1


   Bertrand-type questions have also been studied for number fields more generally. Hulse and Murty

proved results about the existence of prime ideals whose norms lie in intervals of the form


                                 x ≤N(p) ≤BKx


for suitable constants BK > 1 depending on the field K ([Hulse]). Their result is a number-field analogue

of Bertrand’s postulate, but it is formulated in terms of norm intervals.

   The analytic input used in this paper comes from angular distribution results for primes in imaginary

quadratic rings. Classical work of Kubilius studied the distribution of Gaussian primes in sectors ([Kubi]).

More directly, Sittinger states an angular prime-counting theorem for imaginary quadratic rings and

derives the consequence that sufficiently large annular sectors contain prime elements ([Sitt]). This is the

result used in the proof of the main theorem.


2  Preliminaries


Let K be an imaginary quadratic number field, and let OK be its ring of integers. Choose an integral basis


                           OK = Z + Zτ,


where τ ∈C \ R and Im(τ) > 0. Thus, every element of OK can be written uniquely as


                                      a1 + a2τ,     a1, a2 ∈Z.


Geometrically, OK forms a lattice in the complex plane.
   Throughout this paper, for every complex number z ∈C, define N(z) = |z|2.

  A nonzero nonunit π ∈OK is a prime element if the principal ideal (π) is a nonzero prime ideal.

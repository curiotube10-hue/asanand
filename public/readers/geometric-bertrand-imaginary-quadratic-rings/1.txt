                                          Abstract

         Bertrand’s postulate asserts that for every n > 1, there exists a rational prime p with n < p < 2n.

      Fixing an integral basis (1, τ), we define a region Bτ(α, (1 + c)α) using coordinate-wise inequalities

     and norm bounds. Under a thickness condition preventing the normalized basis coordinates of α from

      approaching either coordinate axis, we prove that Bτ(α, (1 + c)α) contains a prime element for large

     enough N(α). The proof identifies an annular sector of uniform angular and radial width in the scaled

      region and applies an asymptotic angular prime distribution theorem for imaginary quadratic rings.
